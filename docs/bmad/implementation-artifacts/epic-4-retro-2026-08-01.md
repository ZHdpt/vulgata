## What Went Well

### 1. Runtime behavior was observed rather than assumed

The team qualified the pinned CodeGraphContext 0.5.1 runtime and corrected the
adapter to its actual HTTP response envelope. Bounded heterogeneous graph
matches replaced the timeout-prone multi-column query shape. This kept the
CodeGraph integration deterministic and aligned it with the deployed provider.

### 2. Prior architectural patterns carried forward

Epic 3's recommendations were applied where they mattered:

- the CodeGraph boundary uses a Core contract with an Infrastructure provider;
- scan access follows established authorization and repository-visibility
  patterns;
- migrations and persistence continued to receive SQLite and PostgreSQL-aware
  verification; and
- orchestration behavior remained outside the deterministic profiling core.

### 3. The expedited exception retained a real quality floor

Story 4.3 used the approved `expedited-mvp` route, but still retained transient
RED-GREEN TDD, combined independent review, security and migration checks,
regression verification, and final Docker evidence. The shorter route reduced
ceremony without accepting a failing test or an unresolved High finding.

### 4. Final remediation was narrow

The closing architecture audit found that a public string-path framework
detector could bypass the prepared snapshot lease. The correction removed the
bypass at the invariant boundary instead of redesigning the scanning system.
The final audit accepted the corrected architecture.

## What Slowed Delivery

### 1. Workflow and review iterations were excessive

Stories 4.1 and especially 4.2 accumulated repeated plan, analysis, review,
evidence, and recovery passes. Some iterations found real defects, but others
re-checked substantially the same boundary or created more evidence mechanics
than the decision required. This increased coordination cost and delayed usable
feedback.

The practical lesson is to select the authorized execution level at story
activation, use its exact gate set, and avoid adding duplicate review stages or
new evidence subsystems. Evidence should support a decision, not become a
parallel product.

### 2. Architecture invariants were tested too late

Several important issues appeared during late review or Epic close rather than
at the accepted-plan boundary: provider-envelope assumptions, a public
detector-path bypass, line-ending-sensitive evidence hashes, and atomic
persistence/lease concerns. They were correctable, but late discovery caused
rework across implementation, tests, evidence, and review.

The practical lesson is to name the sole entry point and critical invariant in
the plan, then make its contract test RED before broad implementation. For the
August 15 slice, likely invariants include atomic persistence, snapshot-lease
ownership, exact Cross-Repository matching, authorization, and migration
compatibility.

### 3. Environment failures obscured product progress

Docker registry/package downloads, proxy routing, base-image availability, and
line-ending normalization repeatedly interrupted verification. These were not
all product defects, but they consumed the same feedback loop. Source-bound
Docker commands and line-ending/hash preflight should be run early enough that
environment failures remain distinguishable from implementation failures.
